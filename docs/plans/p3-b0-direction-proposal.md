### 1.5 What B0 covers and does not cover

Every B0 verdict covers simple, disjoint, snapped contours at zoom 1 and densities of 2-8 leaves per cubic at e = 8 ([verdict scope](p3-b0-feasibility-benchmark-contract.md#5-extrapolation-and-the-feasibility-decision)). It does not cover:

- **Zoom, two effects.**
  - **Leaf growth.** The roadmap's 1%-6400% range is not measured. V3 section c puts flattening growth at roughly 2^(Δ/2) segments for Δ tolerance levels. Zoom 64 against zoom 1 is Δ = 6, about 8 times the leaves (estimate), and at slope about 1 about 8 times the cost.
  - **Rebuild recurrence (DF9).** B0 times one initial build. In use, a zoom-in across a bucket edge repeats a build of every visible path, so B_init-class work also occurs during zoom and pan, which the requirements list as [editing behavior](../requirements.md#editing-behavior) under the 60 fps target (BC13). The prototype plan's P3 exit gate requires that "zoom/DPR changes crossing a bucket rebuild only affected geometry" ([P3 exit gate](../prototype-plan.md#p3-fill-and-stroke-meshes)); in a fully visible scene every path is affected. The R2 decision E2 (parked under D8) builds epoch changes by clipping cached tessellation. Whether that also covers tolerance-bucket crossings, which need new flattening, is not established here.
- **Generic coordinates.** O1: unsnapped paths almost all fail `Topology(KnotMismatch)`, 0-4 of 30 admitted per O1/P2 cell. That failure comes from the exact-knot topology certificate ([B0R section 1.2](p3-b0r-kernel-performance-contract.md#12-topology-code)), the stage a fast rung might replace (Alt-A1, B0D-Q3).
- **Crossings.** O2: every crossing star fails `Topology(Unresolved)`; the transverse arrangement accepts one crossing partner per leaf. Crossing cost is not measured (B0-Q4).
- **WASM and browser.** Both runs are native, single thread, test-harness release build. k = 2 is provisional until B0-W.
- **Edit latency and draw cost.** B0 event E3 is a lower-bound proxy; event E4 is deferred. Neither covers scene application, packing, the fringe builder (DF8), upload or draw.

## 3. Options

Common criteria, per option: the gaps it closes and by how much; what it changes; new dependencies; risk; rough size in work units (one unit is about one plan checkpoint with its own PR, like a B0R unit; all sizes are estimates); and the evidence that would confirm it.

### Alt-A. A faster fill path for interactive paths, with the exact kernel kept

The current kernel does two expensive things that a common-case path may not need: it certifies the continuous cubic family (the hull-pair certificate, 71-72% of time) and it embeds exact sections in a vertical-slab decomposition. A fast rung that triangulates without Steiner points would, on B0's crossing-free corpus, use the flattened input vertices as its mesh vertices and place no new vertex. (Today's kernel also places none on this corpus, but through exact section embedding.)

**Alt-A1, fallback ladder (one output per path).** A new kernel path in Rust:

- It flattens as today, then runs a sweep over all contours of the path together, with exact-sign orientation on the Float64 vertices (the filtered `orient2d` of B0R's F1 filter with its exact fallback already exists). The sweep proves that the flattened contours are simple and mutually non-crossing, and it triangulates the resolved region under the fill rule, for example by monotone decomposition without Steiner points.
- Every decision is exact. Positions are the input vertices, so the mesh region equals the flattened polygon's region exactly.
- When the sweep finds a crossing, a coincidence or any case outside its scope, the path goes to today's exact kernel unchanged, which also serves as the test oracle. The ladder is deterministic: equal requests take the same rung (V1 rule 6).
- It changes `ok` meshes (a different triangulation), so it gets a new `operationId` and revision (BC11). Cache keys then miss once; nothing else in V1-V3 changes.

**The topology argument it needs (BC1).** Exact simplicity of the flattened polygon plus P2's flattening bound is not by itself enough. The private contract rules that "a connectivity change cannot be excused by a small positional bound", and it says that P3.2e's line-fill certificates do not certify "the topology of a preceding cubic flattening stage". Two curves can cross or touch while their flattenings stay disjoint, and the reverse. The fast rung therefore needs a cheap exact sufficient condition that ties the polygon's topology to the curves', for example:

- exact clearance of strictly greater than twice the flattening bound (touching at exactly 2δ would be a connectivity change under BC1) between every pair of non-adjacent flattened edges of different leaves, so that the curves, each within the bound of its polyline, cannot meet; with
- the existing monotone-projection argument for adjacent leaves.

Its cost is unmeasured. It is cheaper in kind than the hull-pair certificate (segment distances on sweep candidates instead of hull intersections), but whether it is cheap enough is what the spike measures. This is B0D-Q3.

**What the certificate choice also decides: admission.** The O1 `KnotMismatch` failures come from the exact-knot topology certificate ([B0R section 1.2](p3-b0r-kernel-performance-contract.md#12-topology-code)) that the fast rung would replace. Today 0-4 of 30 generic-coordinate paths per O1/P2 cell are admitted. B0D-Q3 therefore also decides whether generic-coordinate paths can be admitted at all, which matters more to real documents than speed on snapped ones.

**R0 and R2 (BC18).** A monotone triangulation without Steiner points tends to produce slivers. Under the U2 planning direction, generator-induced slivers are R0's subject (clearance-aware triangulation), and R2's tiling (K1 with E1-E3) is the production carrier that clips meshes per cell. Interpretation: both bind the fast rung as directions. Its triangulation must leave room for a clearance-aware choice, and its output must be clippable under E1-E3. Since D8 parks the certificate line and evidences A04 by measurement (BC17), the spike reports sliver statistics and does not claim R0 or R2 compliance.

**Alt-A2, dual pipeline (interactive mesh, then exact mesh).** A Float64 tessellator with approximate intersection placement draws first; the exact kernel later replaces the mesh and validates it, in idle time or a worker.

- Its interactive output can be wrong in near-degenerate cases, which BC1 and BC3 forbid unless the user changes them for interactive output.
- The swap is a new visible state (BC15) and needs a requirement entry.
- The exact work still costs T̂ in total (21.3-41.4 s at k = 2, DF3), and again at every zoom bucket crossing (DF9). On the main thread a single 256-leaf path (21.3-41.4 ms, DF6) exceeds a frame, so Alt-A2 effectively needs a worker and an asynchronous port (BC10).
- Two meshes per path during the swap: at most about 2 × 15.41 MiB ≈ 31 MiB of payload (estimate), within B_mem. It counts against the 256 MB combined ceiling (BC19) together with the V3 mesh cache budget of 64 MiB and the WASM arenas.

### Alt-B. Requirement and budget changes

**Alt-B1, progressive or asynchronous initial build.** Build meshes over several frames instead of one `prepare`.

- **Arithmetic.** DF6: 41.4 s is about 2,485 full frames at 60 Hz, or about 10,355 frames (about 173 s) with a 4 ms slice. With DF3's 19.1 s it is about 80 s at 4 ms per frame.
- **It recurs on zoom.** By DF9 the same rebuild starts again, for every visible path, at each zoom-in bucket crossing. During it the old meshes are coarser and may not serve (V3 section c; serving them would be silently coarse geometry, BC3), so the paths disappear until rebuilt. The one-to-three-minute outcome recurs in part at each zoom step, scaled by the number of visible paths.
- **Acceptability.** No requirement fixes an initial build time, and B_init is a feasibility budget (BC16). But because of the recurrence, Alt-B1 meets the 60 fps typical-editing target for zoom and pan (BC13) only by leaving paths undrawn. In the Primary's judgment, paths appearing over one to three minutes for a 1,000-path document, again after zooming, is not an acceptable editor experience.
- **It does not fit the frame either.** One 256-leaf path costs 21.3-41.4 ms at k = 2 (DF6), more than a 16.67 ms frame, and today's `attempt` runs to completion. So Alt-B1 needs a resumable kernel attempt or a worker.
- **Changes.** BC10 (V1 rule 3, V3 consumption step 5); a new visible state "not yet built" with a requirement entry (BC15).
- **Gap closed.** None of the edit gap. The build gap is hidden, not closed.
- **Size (estimate).** 5-8 units including a worker or resumable attempt.
- **Evidence.** A time-to-complete-scene, zoom-step and frame-time measurement in a headed browser scenario.

**Alt-B2, incremental edit.** Per-path rebuild is already the edit model: any path-data change gives the node a new geometry revision and rebuilds the whole mesh ([V3 section b](p3-v3-renderer-mesh-pipeline-contract.md#b-port-consumption-geometry-revision-and-cache-key)). So t̂_edit, 122 ms, is already per path. Further localization would be:

- **Per cubic, topology.** Recertify only pairs that involve leaves of the edited cubics. Moving one anchor changes two of 32 cubics, 1/16 of the leaves.
- **Per region, tessellation.** Rebuild only the slabs whose x-range the edited edges touch, and splice the mesh.
- **Bound.** If cost were proportional to changed leaves, at most about 16 times faster for a two-cubic edit (estimate, upper bound). Fixed costs, the sweep and edits that span the path's x-extent reduce it, to no gain in the worst case. Against DF4's edit factor of 7.1-30.5 this might close the gap only for small edits, unmeasured. It does nothing for the zoom recurrence (DF9).
- **Changes.** A new port version with base revision and delta (BC10); kernel state retained across calls, which must stay a reconstructible cache and give results equal to a full rebuild (BC8, V1 rule 6); an incremental fringe builder (DF8); mesh splicing in the renderer cache.
- **Size (estimate).** 8-12 units. **Risk.** High: history-dependent state, splice correctness, and the fringe builder's own cost.
- **Evidence.** A new edit scenario timing real anchor drags, not the proxy.

**Alt-B3, budget revision.** B0-Q1's budgets are the user's feasibility budgets, not requirements (BC16).

- Making the remeasure FEASIBLE would need B_init ≥ 41.4 s and B_edit ≥ 122 ms. The looser B0-Q1 alternative (2,000 ms, 8 ms, 128 MiB) still leaves about 20.7× and 15.3×.
- **B_edit.** Up to one 16.67 ms frame it is still a budget-only change, and it still leaves about 122 / 16.67 ≈ 7.3×. Above a frame it conflicts with "typical editing targets 60 frames per second" for path editing (BC13); that is a requirement change, not a budget change.
- **B_init.** No requirement fixes an initial load time, but by DF9 B_init-class work recurs at zoom bucket crossings in a fully visible scene, under the same 60 fps target. A higher B_init therefore also accepts longer zoom stalls, unless a separate decision bounds zoom rebuilds. It is the user's call, decided before a measurement and with a stated reason, so the result does not redefine its own threshold (BC16). Under the B0 contract it is a new decision on B0-Q1, and a new scenario version if the decision rule changes.
- **Size.** 1 unit. **Gap closed.** None technically.

### Alt-C. B0R2, a further bit-identical kernel round

- **Ceiling.** DF5: even a free topology stage leaves about 5.6 s for the workload; even free exact work speeds the whole by at most 1.16. FEASIBLE at k = 2 needs about 19-41 times overall (DF4) on code whose outcomes, triangulation and charge sequence must stay bit-identical, so the algorithm and its asymptotics stay.
- **Why 41× is unlikely.** B0R's gains came from one hotspot: `multiply<34,34,68>` was 71.3% of a path's time in the first record ([B0R section 1.1](p3-b0r-kernel-performance-contract.md#11-stage-split-and-routine-attribution)). The remeasure shows no comparable single hotspot at the granularity the record has; the residual exact share is 0.13, and the rest is spread over loop machinery, copies and certificate checks the record does not attribute.
- **Primary's estimate (unmeasured):** a further 2-4×, giving T̂ about 10-21 s, still 10-21 × B_init.
- **Changes.** None. **Dependencies.** None. **Size (estimate).** 6-9 units, like B0R. **Risk.** Low correctness risk under the B0R oracles, high risk of an INFEASIBLE remeasure. It also leaves O1 admission unchanged.
- **Evidence.** The same B0 v1 remeasure.

### Alt-D. Rerun to resolve INCONCLUSIVE

- **What it would change.** Only check 2. Both failing fits are P8 p95 fits of individually timed paths (s_e 0.222 and 0.224); every median fit passes (at most 1.742). A run without those elevated samples would likely pass check 2 and turn the verdict into INFEASIBLE.
- **What it would not change.** The direction. The median fits alone give T̂ = 21.3-41.4 s (DF3), and the measured 64-leaf cells alone give about 4.8 s at λ = 2 (DF2). No plausible rerun reaches FEASIBLE. Under B0-Q3, INCONCLUSIVE is already handled the same way as INFEASIBLE: it goes to the user, as it has.
- **Limits.** The record does not identify the source of the elevated samples, and the user's standing instruction excludes CPU-load sampling, so a rerun cannot diagnose them either.
- **Size.** 1 unit: about 2.5 minutes of run time, a record and an evidence review.

### Alt-E. Combinations

- **Alt-E1 (recommended): Alt-A1, gated by a measured spike, with the budgets, Alt-B1 and Alt-B2 unchanged for now.** The spike is test-only and measures the ladder on the B0 corpus under the B0 fits and budgets before any production contract. It is consistent with the private contract's rule that "a non-accepting executable spike needs its own frozen inputs, expected membership, limits and output artifacts before execution" ([fill topology](p3-private-contract.md#fill-topology-and-independent-verification)).
- **Alt-E2: Alt-A1 plus Alt-B2.** Only if the spike meets B_init but not B_edit. Alt-B2's cost is high, so it should wait for that evidence.
- **Alt-E3: Alt-A1 plus workers (section 4).** Only if the spike lands within a small factor of B_init. DF7 bounds what workers give: about 8-10 times realistically, 16 at most, on the reference machine, and nothing for a single edit.
- **Not considered further: a GPU path renderer** (stencil-then-cover or compute rasterization) instead of WASM meshes. It would replace the accepted rule that arbitrary paths use WASM-produced meshes ([graphics architecture, primitive rendering](../graphics-engine-architecture.md#primitive-rendering)), the A5 fringe decision and V3. It is listed only so that its absence is deliberate.

## 4. Worker threads and WASM threads

**Scope.** Neither the requirements nor `ARCHITECTURE.md` mention workers or threads. Three documents constrain them:

- The P2 private contract: "One single-threaded WASM instance owns one arena. No imported browser objects, shared memory, callbacks or reentrant calls" ([P2 private contract, ABI v1](p2-private-contract.md#abi-v1-and-memory-lifetime)).
- V1 rule 7: the fill port is synchronous, and a worker or asynchronous port is a new port version ([V1 section b](p3-v1-path-scene-port-proposal.md#b-fill-mesh-port-in-packagescontracts)).
- The B0 contract measures a single thread ([event E1](p3-b0-feasibility-benchmark-contract.md#1-measured-events)).

So threads are not excluded by a requirement, but they are outside the current architecture and port contract.

**Plain Web Workers**, each with its own single-threaded WASM instance and no shared memory, fit BC12 as V2 reads it (one engine per instance). They need an asynchronous port version, a host-composition change recorded in an ADR, worker lifetime and disposal rules, and copies or transfers of requests and results. No dependency is needed. Each instance has its own arena: V2's output arena never shrinks and may reach 256 MiB ([V3 section d](p3-v3-renderer-mesh-pipeline-contract.md#d-mesh-cache-failure-memo-and-their-bounds)), so worker count and arena reserves count against the 256 MB combined ceiling (BC19).

**WASM threads with shared memory** need `SharedArrayBuffer`, which needs cross-origin isolation of the host page. For an embeddable SDK ([requirements, product goal](../requirements.md#product-goal)) that is a requirement on every host's headers. They would also change BC12 and would need a dependency approval if they use a thread-pool crate (BC5).

**What threads give.** DF7: about 8-10 times for the build on the reference machine realistically and 16 at most, so 2.6-5.2 × B_init at best, and nothing for the latency of one edited path. The edited path would still lag the pointer by 28-122 ms at k = 2 (DF4), that is, update at about 8-35 Hz (estimate), off the main thread. The prototype plan requires representative workloads on lower-tier hardware before alpha ([requirement coverage](../prototype-plan.md#requirement-coverage)), and such hardware may have fewer cores.

Threads are a lever after a faster algorithm, not a replacement for one.

## 5. Recommendation (Primary)

This is the Primary's recommendation; the user decides.

**Adopt Alt-E1:** pursue Alt-A1, the fallback ladder, and gate it by a measured, test-only spike before any production contract. Keep the B0-Q1 budgets, keep the D8 checkpoint B1 (kernel caps) blocked, do not run Alt-D on its own, and defer Alt-A2, Alt-B1, Alt-B2 and threads until the spike's result.

Reasons:

1. **It targets what the records measure.** 99.4-99.7% of a path's time at the largest cells is the topology certificate and the slab tessellation (section 1.2). Alt-C cannot remove them under bit identity (DF5), and Alt-B1 and Alt-B3 do not remove them at all, including at each zoom step (DF9).
2. **It keeps the exactness constraints.** Alt-A1 decides topology exactly (BC1), places no new vertices on the common case, keeps the exact kernel for crossings and degeneracies (BC4) and needs no dependency (BC5). Its one open point is the topology sufficient condition that ties the flattened polygon to the curves (B0D-Q3). D8's measurement method covers its geometric error (A04) but not its topology (BC17), so that condition needs a proof and independent review.
3. **It may also admit generic coordinates.** The certificate it replaces is the source of the O1 `KnotMismatch` failures (section 1.5). No other option admits them exactly.
4. **It changes no requirement.** The port, the cache and the renderer stay as V1-V3 fixed them; only the operation identity changes (BC11).
5. **It is falsifiable cheaply.** The spike reuses the B0 corpus, fits and budgets. If it misses, the user decides again with a measured number instead of an estimate, and Alt-E2 or Alt-E3 become concrete.
6. **Alt-D does not change the direction** (section 3, Alt-D), and under B0-Q3 the INCONCLUSIVE verdict has already gone to the user.

**What the recommendation does not solve.** The fringe builder's cost (DF8) is outside the kernel and outside B0. V4's existing gate on it stays, and the R2/K1 placement decision still owns it. The zoom recurrence (DF9) is reduced only as far as the fast rung is fast; a zoom-step scenario is a later item. B0-W's WASM factor, the zoom range, generic-coordinate cost and crossings remain unmeasured (section 7).

**Next work units under Alt-E1**, each with its own PR:

1. A full-tier spike contract: a new scenario identity on the B0 corpus bytes, the ladder's rungs and fallback accounting, the fast rung's topology sufficient condition, the mesh and region oracles, an O1 admission observation, sliver statistics, the same fits, budgets and decision rule. Independent review, then the user's approval.
2. The test-only spike and its reference run with a committed record.
3. Then, depending on the verdict: a production contract for Alt-A1, or a new user decision among Alt-E2, Alt-E3 and Alt-B.

## 7. Risks and unknowns

- **The spike may miss.** 1.95 µs per leaf native is about 19 times below today's 37.3 µs (DF1). No committed record measures an alternative tessellator. If the spike misses, the user is back at this decision, with a measured number.
- **The topology condition may cost or reject too much.** The clearance condition of B0D-Q3 (a) is unproved and unmeasured. It may reject many paths whose curves are close but disjoint, which then take the slow exact rung.
- **Admission of generic coordinates.** Today 0-4 of 30 unsnapped paths per O1/P2 cell are admitted, because of the exact-knot certificate (section 1.5). Whether the fast rung's condition admits them is unknown; if not, real documents mostly take the exact rung or fail.
- **The fallback rate is unknown.** B0's corpus is snapped and crossing-free. Real paths with crossings (O2) take the exact rung; its cost then still applies to them.
- **Zoom recurrence.** Every option's build cost recurs at zoom-in bucket crossings in a fully visible scene (DF9). B0 measures one build only; a zoom-step scenario is still needed.
- **Slivers.** A Steiner-free monotone triangulation may create slivers, the subject of R0 under the U2 direction (BC18). The spike reports them; it does not settle R0.
- **WASM factor.** k = 2 is provisional until B0-W. Every figure here scales with it; the ratio could be above or below 2.
- **Zoom range.** B0 measures zoom 1 only. Zoom 64 may need about 8 times the leaves (section 1.5, estimate) and, at slope about 1, about 8 times the cost. The 0.01 zoom end may need fewer.
- **Extrapolation.** L\* = 256 is four times the largest measured L. The curvature guard limits, but does not remove, a new cost regime above L = 64. DF2 avoids extrapolation for λ = 2 only.
- **The record's noise.** The source of the elevated P8 p95 samples is not identified, and Alt-D cannot identify it without CPU-load sampling, which the user's standing instruction excludes.
- **Memory.** Dual meshes (Alt-A2) and per-worker WASM arenas (section 4) count against the 256 MB combined ceiling (BC19), which no option has measured.
- **Outside the kernel.** The fringe builder (DF8, anecdotal 141 ms at 256 vertices), scene application, packing, upload and draw (B0 event E4) are not in B0. A FEASIBLE kernel does not make edits fit a frame by itself.
