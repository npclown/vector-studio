# D9 E14-U1: a separate edge cap for the fill fast rung

Status: **APPROVED 2026-10-24** (independent review round 3 READY, optional wording fix applied; [section 12](#12-review-record)), documentation only. Questions E14-Q1 to E14-Q3 are answered by their recommended option (a), adopted automatically on 2026-10-24 under the user's standing instruction ([section 10](#10-questions-and-decisions)).

This is rendering-track item E14 ([D9 rendering track](d9-rendering-track.md#status-index)), unit E14-U1, the fix direction F1 of the [E14 cause record](../evidence/e14-fill-work-limit-cause-2026-10-24.md#fix-direction). The user moved E14 to phase R1 on 2026-10-24, with E2, because fill `work-limit` on ordinary smooth paths would fail A02 in the P3 exit sweep. It is full tier: it changes fill output, the fill operation id and kernel caps. It changes no threshold of the [P3 acceptance table](p3-fill-stroke-meshes.md#runtime-acceptance-coverage-to-freeze-in-p30b), no tolerance, no share of the 0.25 px budget and no stroke cap. Identifiers use the prefix **E14-**. Stroke is E2's ([E2 contract](d9-e2-stroke-fast-rung-contract.md)).

## 1. Cause and what this contract changes

- **Cause** ([record](../evidence/e14-fill-work-limit-cause-2026-10-24.md#summary)). All 641 fill `work-limit` rows of the E11-U2 scene `p3-exit-stress/v1` are the S1 prefix's flattened-edge cap `MAX_EDGE_OWNERS = 256` (`cubic_fill.rs`): 638 `CommandLimit` and 3 `OwnerLimit`. The prefix rejects the path before rung 0 or the exact rung does any work. At the V3 tolerance (1/16 to 1/8 device px), a smooth path needs more than 256 edges from about 32 to 64 device px of radius.
- **One cap, two rungs.** Rung 0 ([Alt-A1 §3.4](p3-alt-a1-fill-ladder-contract.md#34-caps-storage-and-allocation)) runs inside the prefix caps, and the exact rung's `FILL_V0_LIMITS` are derived from the same constant. The exact rung's pair and section budgets are quadratic in it (E = 4,096 would mean about 33M sections), but rung 0's stages are not.
- **Change.** Rung 0 and the S1 prefix run that feeds it get their own edge cap **E0 = 8,192** (E14-K1). The exact rung keeps E = 256 and every byte of its behavior (E14-K2). Rung 0 gains a work cap so that its cost stays bounded at the new size (E14-K3). Fill becomes `geometry-wasm/fill-v0+r3`.
- **Not changed.** The flattener, the tolerance buckets (F3 and F4 of the record, rejected there), `MAX_CONTOURS` 4, `MAX_SOURCE_SEGMENTS` 64, rung 0's stages FR1 to FR8 and AL-FR9 and their order, the exact rung's cascade, every stroke cap and the stroke rung instance of E2-K3.

## 2. Terms

- **E** = 256, the exact rung's edge cap: `MAX_EDGE_OWNERS` today, and every quantity of `FILL_V0_LIMITS` derived from it.
- **E0** = 8,192, rung 0's edge cap. **Flat edges** of a path: the edges of its S1 polygon, `MAX_EDGE_OWNERS`'s unit. Flat commands = flat edges + MOVE and CLOSE verbs, so a rung-0 path has at most E0 + 2·4 flat commands.
- **Today** means `fill-v0+r2` on `main` 8d98285.

## 3. E14-K1: rung 0's edge cap

1. **Constants.** `cubic_fill.rs` gains `MAX_FAST_EDGE_OWNERS = 8,192` beside `MAX_EDGE_OWNERS = 256`, with `MAX_FAST_FLAT_COMMANDS` and `MAX_FAST_CONTOUR_POINTS` derived from it as today's are from E. They are fill constants: nothing in `stroke_*.rs` reads them, and E2-K3's stroke instance keeps its own caps (E2 §6, "Instance").
2. **Prefix caps per call.** The workspace's prefix arrays (`commands`, `contour_points`, `edge_owners`) are sized for E0. Each prefix run enforces the caps of its caller, passed per call instead of read from the one `caps` field: `fast_rung_until` calls `prepare_prefix` with the workspace's **fast caps** and the matching command capacity; the four `attempt*` entries keep the workspace's exact caps (`B1_CAPS`) and `MAX_FLAT_COMMANDS`, and so does `certify_topology`, whose `leaves` bound (`cubic_fill.rs`, `self.caps.edge_owners`) stays the exact one. A prefix run over the fast caps is rung 0's `PREFIX`, as an over-cap prefix is today.
   - **Fast caps by mode.** A production workspace (`with_caps(B1_CAPS)`) has fast caps E0 with `MAX_FAST_FLAT_COMMANDS` and `MAX_FAST_CONTOUR_POINTS`. The test-only pre-B1 workspace (`new_pre_b1`) has fast caps equal to `PRE_B1_CAPS`, so the AL-I2, AL-I3, AL-F7 and AL-S1 harnesses that run in that mode ([P3 plan, B1 kernel caps](p3-fill-stroke-meshes.md#b1-kernel-caps)) see exactly today's rung 0.
3. **Rung 0's arrays.** `fill_ladder.rs`'s `MAX_POINTS`, `MAX_EDGES` and the capacities derived from them (Alt-A1 §3.4) follow E0. `MAX_SOURCES` and `MAX_CONTOURS` are unchanged. Spike [§2.5](p3-b0-spike-ladder-contract.md)'s triangle bound n + 2h − 2k still sizes the triangle array, so a capacity overflow stays `TRIANGLE`.
4. **Storage.** At E0 the fixed arrays no longer fit a WebAssembly stack frame (1 MiB by default): `size_of::<FastRung>()` extrapolates from the record's two sizes (113,240 bytes at 256, 1,726,040 at 4,096) to about 3.4 MB. Therefore:
   - `FastRung` is constructed in place in heap storage, never on a stack: `FillLadder::reserve` and every test that builds one (today `Box::new(FastRung::new())` in `fill_ladder_tests.rs` and `fill_ladder_dump_tests.rs`) use one fallible heap constructor.
   - The prefix arrays at E0 (about 590 KB: `FlatCommand` 40 bytes × 8,200 plus two arrays of 16 bytes × about 8,192) leave the inline `CubicFillWorkspace` struct, which `with_caps` builds on the stack and `build_workspace` moves through `Result`. They become heap buffers reserved fallibly at construction and counted in `allocated_bytes` and the `MAX_COMBINED_HEAP_BYTES` check.
   - The stage-local arrays sized by the caps move into a rung-0 scratch reserved once at `fill_v0_reserve`: FR5's `boxes`, `order` and `scratch`; FR7's `next`, `previous`, `order`, `scratch` (sized `MAX_POINTS`), `rank`, `kind`, `active`, `helper`, `diagonals`, `visited`, `face`, `is_left`, the second `scratch` (sized `MAX_HALF_EDGES`), `sorted` and `stack`; AL-FR9's `next` and `seen`. FR7's whole-array copy `let vertices = self.vertices;` becomes a borrow. A stage reads and writes only the prefix `[..n]` of each, and first sets that prefix to the value today's declaration gives it (`false`, `0` or the default), so nothing from an earlier path is read and no call touches a full-capacity array. This is the same move as E2 §6 plans for the generic rung; E14-U1 does it for the fill instance, and E2-U3 builds on it.
   - FR5's candidate bitset, which feeds only a `debug_assert!` and is quadratic in E0 (8 MiB at E0), is reserved only in builds with debug assertions and clears only the rows it set.
5. **The two scratch checks.** The fill scratch that AL-FR9 reuses is reserved for `max(3·(T + 2V) of FILL_V0_LIMITS, MAX_BOUNDARY_RECORDS)` records, where `MAX_BOUNDARY_RECORDS` = 3·(E0 + 10) = 24,606 (`MAX_TRIANGLES` = `MAX_POINTS` + 2·4 − 2). `FillLadder::reserve` takes the workspace's fast caps and checks the records those caps need, so a pre-B1 ladder (`PRE_B1_FILL_V0_LIMITS`, 2,304 records) keeps passing its check. The exact rung's own capacity checks (`refine`'s `triangles.capacity()`, `collect_boundary`'s `edges` and `boundary` capacities in `fill_export.rs`) are bounded by `FillScratch::sizes(FILL_V0_LIMITS)` instead of the grown `capacity()`, so an overflow that is a self-check failure at `r2` stays one.
6. **Allocation rules unchanged.** Every byte is reserved once with fallible reservation (refusal is `BATCH_ALLOCATION_FAILED`), counted in the reserve-time totals and `linearMemoryBytes()`; `fill_v0_process` allocates nothing and moves no storage. E14-U1 records the total reserved bytes of the fill engine before and after; the growth (rung 0's storage and scratch, the prefix buffers and the fill scratch) must be at most 16 MiB (E14-G3).

## 4. E14-K2: the exact rung keeps E

- **Identity.** The exact rung is the one path to a mesh for every path rung 0 refuses, at every size. Its four `attempt*` entries stay bit-identical to the merge base (Alt-A1 [AL-I1](p3-alt-a1-fill-ladder-contract.md#44-identity-rules-and-the-re-baseline)), and on every path where rung 0 falls back, the export output equals today's (AL-I2).
- **What a large refused path gets.** A path with more than E flat edges that rung 0 refuses (`MONOTONE`, `CROSSING`, `CLEARANCE` or any other reason) reaches the exact rung, whose prefix returns today's `CommandLimit` or `OwnerLimit`, so the port result is today's `work-limit`. E14-U1 creates no new port outcome.
- **`FILL_V0_LIMITS`** is unchanged and keeps reading `MAX_EDGE_OWNERS`. The record's experiment raised it with the prefix (holding only pairs and sections at E), which is why looping paths there failed with `Topology(WorkLimit)` instead of `CommandLimit`. E14-U1 does not do that.

## 5. E14-K3: rung 0's work cap

Rung 0 has no work budget today, because at E = 256 its scans are small. Three loops are not linear in the edge count:

- FR5's x sweep visits every pair whose x intervals overlap within `r = 2δ`. It reaches n²/2 when many edges share one x window, for example near-vertical flat edges of tall cubics. With 64 sources, a polyline comb has at most 64 edges, so such a path is built from cubics.
- FR7's status search (`left_edge`) and `remove_edge`'s shift scan the active edge list per event. The list holds about two edges per y-monotone chain, so it stays short on smooth paths.
- FR7's face tracing calls `next_half` once per half-edge, and `next_half` scans **every** diagonal for the ones at its vertex. That is about 3n·n, quadratic on every admitted path, rings included: about 200M visits at E0. The record's 2 µs per edge was a median over paths of about 1,400 edges.

**Indexing.** E14-U1 gives `next_half` a per-vertex diagonal index, built once per triangulation in O(n) by a counting sort that keeps index order. `next_half` then calls `consider` on `w` and on the diagonals at `w` in the same order as today, so its choice and its `TRIANGLE` errors are unchanged.

- **Cap.** One counter per call. It is charged before each visit: once per FR5 sweep candidate, once per active edge that `left_edge` visits or `remove_edge` shifts, and once per `consider` call. At **W0 = 4,194,304** (2^22) charges, the next charge stops rung 0 with the new fallback reason `WORK`, and the exact rung runs as on any fallback. W0 is inclusive.
- **Unreachable at E.** With at most E flat edges, FR5 has at most E(E−1)/2 = 32,640 candidates. FR7 has at most (E + 4)² = 67,600 status visits and as many shifts. Face tracing has at most H·(D + 1) `consider` calls, with H = 798 half-edges and D = 269 diagonals at E. The total is below 400,000, under W0, so `WORK` cannot occur and every path rung 0 handles today has the same outcome.
- **Observability.** `WORK` is a native-only reason like the other ten (Alt-A1 §3.6): no port, framing or adapter change. It is appended last to `Reason` and `Reason::ALL`. The tests that read the `MESH_CHECK` count as `reasons[Reason::ALL.len()]` (`fill_ladder_tests.rs`) name `Reason::MeshCheck` instead. The TypeScript `LADDER_REASONS` list (`tests/geometry/p3-fill-ladder/dump.ts`) gains `WORK`, and the AL-S1 harness and the identity tool print it.
- **Fill only.** W0 and `WORK` belong to the fill instance. E2-U3's stroke instance (4,096 sources) sets its own work bound in its own unit.

## 7. Gates

- **E14-G1, outcomes (deterministic).** With `pnpm test:p3-fill-identity --failure-to-success --revision-change --base <merge base>`, every case is identical to `r2` or moves from `work-limit` to `ok`. No `ok` row changes, and no row moves to `unsupported-geometry`, `numeric-range` or an internal error. Every `ok` row of the scene at `r2` stays `ok`. Today the tool rejects that combination (`--revision-change and --failure-to-success exclude each other`), and its failure-to-success mode keeps the equal-revision check. E14-U1 lets the two flags combine: the per-record failure-to-success classification, with `checkRevisions` requiring branch = base + 1. The tool's self-test gains that combination. **Size records.** Moving the prefix buffers to the heap and adding rung-0 scratch changes the two size records `meta/cubic-workspace-sizes` (`size_of::<CubicFillWorkspace>()` and `allocated_bytes()`) and `meta/fill-engine-sizes`, which the tool requires equal in failure-to-success mode and which revision-change mode exempts only for the engine record. In the combined mode both records are compared as recorded changes, printed base against branch, not as FAIL; every other META field and every output record keeps its rule. This sets aside, for E14-U1 only, AL-I1's workspace-size identity (B0R [§2](p3-b0r-kernel-performance-contract.md#2-outcome-preservation-rule), row "Every workspace": `allocated_bytes()`, `size_of` of inline parts), because the exact rung's prefix storage cannot stay inline at E0 (section 3, item 4). The rest of that row, zero allocations inside an attempt, still holds, and E14-G3 bounds the growth.
- **E14-G2, cost (diagnostic A/B, native release, one machine).** On the paths rung 0 admits at `r2` (the AL-I3 corpus and the scene's rung-0 meshes), the per-path median time of `build_path` at `r3` is at most 1.10 × the `r2` median, per corpus. On the paths that fall back at `r2` with at most E flat edges, the same bound holds. A failure blocks the unit. It is a gate, not a performance claim.
- **E14-G3, memory.** Rung 0's reserved bytes (the `FastRung` storage, the rung-0 scratch and the fill scratch growth) are at most 16 MiB and are recorded with the fill engine's total before and after. `process_does_not_allocate_or_move_storage` passes on E14-F2, E14-F3, E14-F5 and E14-F8.
- **E14-G4, claims.** E14 makes no speed claim. A claim needs a committed record under [`docs/benchmarks/README.md`](../benchmarks/README.md).

## 10. Questions and decisions

**E14-Q1. May the E11-Q2 closed list take a narrow class for large self-intersecting fill paths?**

- (a) **Recommended.** Yes, after E14-U1 has merged, the class **"fill `work-limit` on a self-crossing path over E"**: a rejected fill row is in it when (1) its S1 polygon at the row's tolerance has more than E flat edges, and (2) two edges of that polygon cross properly, decided by exact `orient2d` (strictly opposite signs at both edges), and (3) its port result is `work-limit`. The A01 sweep applies the test with its own exact predicates on the P2 flattening of the row, not the generator's label, and counts the class per stratum (E11 §7.2). Fixture E14-F5, backlog item E14-U2. A polygon crossing can also come from two parts of a simple curve that pass within the flattening tolerance of each other; the class takes those too, and the A01 record reports them. For that class only, this sets aside the private contract's "named ordinary fixtures must succeed" and "Do not … reduce the ordinary-success corpus" ([P3 private contract](p3-private-contract.md)), and the P3 plan's "Ordinary frozen corpus must succeed; prevent blanket NUMERIC_RANGE/WORK_LIMIT from masking unsupported geometry" ([P3 plan](p3-fill-stroke-meshes.md), bounded-work rule), as E11-Q2 (a) did for the stroke class. E11 §7.2's list of classes is amended accordingly (section 6). It does not cover E14-F6 (over E0) or E14-F7 (simple, near-touching): such rows still fail A02.
- (b) No class. E14-U2 becomes R1 and must merge before the exit: a new exact-rung algorithm with its own certificate, which puts the exit behind the largest change of the record.
- (c) A class for every fill `work-limit` over E. It masks simple paths such as E14-F7, which the text quoted in (a) forbids.

**E14-Q2. The value of E0.** (a) **Recommended.** 8,192. The E3 large class reaches 8,192 px of physical extent at tolerances down to 1/16 px ([E3 size classes](d9-e3-b4-precision-contract.md)), about 4,096 to 5,800 px of radius for a centered path, while the record's experiment at 4,096 already meshed every smooth scene path below 8,192 px of radius; edges grow as the square root of size, so 8,192 adds about a factor of four in size. It also equals P2's per-cubic line cap. (b) 4,096, the measured value: half the memory, no margin above the large class for noisier shapes than the scene's.

**E14-Q3. Rung 0's work cap.** (a) **Recommended.** E14-K3 with W0 = 2^22, unreachable at E. (b) No cap: a comb of E0 edges costs about 33M orientation tests in FR5 before falling back.

## 11. Risks
