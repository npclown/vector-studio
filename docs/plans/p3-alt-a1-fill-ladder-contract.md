## 3. The ladder in production

### 3.1 Rungs and stages

For each path that reaches V2's stage 4, the ladder runs rung 0. If rung 0 publishes, its mesh is the path's result. Otherwise rung 0 returns one fallback reason, and rung 1, V2's exact cascade, runs on the same input; its result, mesh or failure, is the path's result. There is one output per path, and the ladder is deterministic (V1 rule 6): rung 0's checks are pure functions of the request in a fixed order.

Rung 0 is the spike's fast rung, [stages FR1-FR8](p3-b0-spike-ladder-contract.md#23-fast-rung-algorithm), with two production additions, AL-FR0 and AL-FR9, and one strengthened closure condition, AL-M5:

| Stage  | Work                                                                                                                 | Reason on failure       |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| AL-FR0 | Range: the request's tolerance exponent e satisfies −511 ≤ e ≤ 510 (section 3.2)                                     | `RANGE`                 |
| S1     | The production S1 prefix (section 1.2), with δ = 2^e                                                                 | `PREFIX`                |
| FR1    | Structure, as the spike                                                                                              | `STRUCTURE`             |
| FR2    | Per-edge deviation δ̄(e) < δ, as the spike                                                                            | `DEVIATION`             |
| FR3    | Micro-closure: SL-M1 to SL-M4 as the spike, then AL-M5 (section 3.3)                                                 | `CLOSURE`               |
| FR4    | Consecutive pairs, SL-C2, as the spike                                                                               | `MONOTONE`              |
| FR5    | Non-consecutive pairs, SL-C3 with r = 2δ = 2^(e+1), as the spike                                                     | `CROSSING`, `CLEARANCE` |
| FR6    | Orientation, nesting, winding and boundary contours under the request's fill rule (nonzero or evenodd), as the spike | `STRUCTURE`             |
| FR7    | Monotone decomposition and stack triangulation, as the spike                                                         | `TRIANGLE`              |
| FR8    | Vertex and triangle assembly, as the spike                                                                           | none                    |
| AL-FR9 | Always-on output check and boundary derivation (section 3.5)                                                         | `MESH_CHECK`            |

Rung 0 returns the first failing reason in stage order, and within a stage in edge or sweep order, as the [spike's fallback rule](p3-b0-spike-ladder-contract.md#26-fallback-rule-and-accounting) fixes. The production code keeps the spike's stage and check order exactly. Section 5.1 forbids an optimization from changing it.

**Line-only paths.** The ladder runs on every path, with or without a `CUBIC` verb, under AL-Q4 (a). On a line-only path, `Line` and close edges have exact source segments, so SL-C1 holds whenever the emitted knots are the source points. SL-C3's clearance above 2δ still applies: a line polygon with features closer than 2δ falls back to the exact rung, which then admits it as today.

### 3.2 Tolerance range of the fast rung (AL-FR0)

The spike ran at δ = 2^-3 only. In production δ = 2^e for every e that V2 accepts (−1021 ≤ e ≤ 1023, [V2 section d](p3-v2-fill-mesh-transport-contract.md#d-tolerance)). The certificate compares against δ² and r² = (2δ)². The spike encloses both by widened interval multiplication (`Interval::point(δ).mul(Interval::point(δ))`, and likewise for r), which is sound at every exponent by R1 to R3: an overflow or a NaN leaves the comparison undecided, so it falls back. Soundness therefore does not need a range.

**AL-FR0 is a conservative restriction, not a soundness condition.** It confines rung 0 to −511 ≤ e ≤ 510, where δ, r, δ² = 2^(2e) and r² = 2^(2e+2) are all normal binary64 values. That keeps the fast rung inside the regime the spike's fixtures, corpus and reviews covered, and away from subnormal and overflowing thresholds whose undecided cases nobody has examined. Outside the range, rung 0 returns `RANGE` before any work, and the exact rung decides as today.

**Same enclosures as the spike.** Inside the range, production computes δ², r² and every other threshold enclosure exactly as the spike does, with the same `Interval` operations in the same order. It does not substitute an exact power of two built from exponent bits, because a narrower threshold interval could prove a comparison the spike leaves undecided, and AL-I3 would then fail.

The renderer requests e from `floor(log2(0.125 / sigmaMax(S)))` ([V3 section c](p3-v3-renderer-mesh-pipeline-contract.md#c-tolerance-and-fringe-buckets)). Leaving the range would need sigmaMax(S) above about 2^508 or below about 2^-513, far beyond the 1%-6400% zoom range. δ itself is built from exponent bits, as V2 builds its tolerances.

### 3.3 The strengthened micro-closure condition AL-M5

The spike record's SL-O6 observation shows a gap. On a contracted contour, the certificate bounds the contracted polygon P′. The mesh, however, triangulates P, whose real edges [p, k] and [k, m] differ from the source curve by up to δ̄(e′) + |k − m| ([record, SL-O6](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-18_p3_p3-b0-spike-ladder-v1_native_desktop-dkvusav_20261018T213619Z-89016cc9.md#sl-o6-geometric-error-observation)). SL-M2 bounds |k − m| < δ, so the published boundary is certified only within 2δ, not within δ. The 0.125 curve share needs the published boundary within δ of the curve.

**AL-M5.** For each degenerate-source close edge, after SL-M1 to SL-M4: δ̄(e′) + |k − m| < δ is proved. Both terms enter as upper bounds: the square root of the interval upper bound of the squared norm (of d_m for δ̄(e′), as FR2 encloses it, and of k − m, as SL-M2 encloses it), widened outward by R1. IEEE 754 square root is correctly rounded, so R1 applies to it as to the other operations. The two upper bounds are added as intervals (R2), so the sum's upper endpoint is rounded outward, and the strict comparison with δ's enclosure is decided by R3; equality or an undecided result fails. Failure is `CLOSURE`.

_Why it suffices._ The map (1 − u) p + u m ↦ (1 − u) p + u k takes e′ = [p, m] onto [p, k] and moves each point by u |k − m| ≤ |k − m|; [k, m] lies within |k − m| of its endpoint m ∈ e′. Every point of the triangle T = conv(p, k, m) also lies within |k − m| of e′ (the [SL-L5 proof](p3-b0-spike-ladder-contract.md#36-closure-and-the-knot-and-endpoint-joins) writes it as a point of e′ plus β (k − m)). The curve piece γ_e′ lies within δ̄(e′) of e′ in the parametric sense (section 3.3 of the spike). So each point of [p, k] ∪ [k, m] is within δ̄(e′) + |k − m| of γ_e′. Conversely, each point of γ_e′ is within δ̄(e′) of some point (1 − u) p + u m of e′, whose image (1 − u) p + u k on [p, k] is within a further |k − m|. That gives a two-sided Hausdorff bound below δ. The topology argument is the spike's, unchanged; AL-M5 only restricts admission. In the spike record, the largest certified O1 bound (0.09375…) plus the largest |k − m| (9.5·10^-16) is below δ, so AL-M5 is expected to reject no O1 path of that corpus; AL-I3 lists any it does. The reviewer confirms this argument (AL-OB1).

AL-M5 is the only certificate change from the spike. It is a strengthening: every path the production rung admits, the spike's rung admits too.

### 3.4 Caps, storage and allocation

- **Caps.** Rung 0 runs inside the caps its S1 prefix enforces (`cubic_fill.rs`'s `MAX_EDGE_OWNERS`, `MAX_CONTOUR_POINTS`, `MAX_CONTOURS`, `MAX_SOURCE_SEGMENTS`). Its array capacities are derived from those constants at compile time, so a B1 cap raise resizes them with the prefix. Triangles are at most n + 2h − 2k (spike section 2.5), and the diagonal array is sized likewise. A capacity overflow is `TRIANGLE`, so a capacity limit costs admission only. Update 2026-10-25 (E14-U1, fill `r3`): rung 0's S1 prefix runs at its own caps, E0 = 8,192 flat edges (`MAX_FAST_EDGE_OWNERS`, with commands and contour points derived as before), while the exact rung keeps E = 256; rung 0's arrays are heap buffers sized for E0 at reserve, and a work cap W0 = 2^22 adds the reason `WORK`, appended last ([E14 contract](d9-e14-fill-rung0-edge-cap-contract.md)).
- **Storage.** The fast rung's workspace (the spike's `FastRung`; the spike record measured `size_of::<SpikeLadder>()` at 41,840 bytes) is allocated once at `fill_v0_reserve`, with fallible reservation, beside the `CubicFillWorkspace` and V2's fill scratch. It is not a stack frame. A reservation failure maps to `ALLOCATION_FAILED`, as V2's [memory rules](p3-v2-fill-mesh-transport-contract.md#memory-and-allocation) do. Its bytes are included in the reserve-time checked totals and in `linearMemoryBytes()`.
- **Boundary scratch.** AL-FR9 reuses V2's directed-edge and boundary scratch, which holds 3 · (T + 2V) records under the rounded `LIMITS`. A fast mesh needs at most 3 · (n + 2h) records. The reserve-time check asserts that this fits.
- **No allocation in a call.** `fill_v0_process` allocates nothing and moves no storage, on both rungs. AL-U1 adds a new allocation test of rung 0 at module level. AL-U3 extends the existing native test `process_does_not_allocate_or_move_storage` to fast-admitted paths and to every fallback reason, once rung 0 is reachable through the export (AL-A6).

### 3.5 Output form of a fast mesh, and the always-on check (AL-FR9)

V2's [section g](p3-v2-fill-mesh-transport-contract.md#g-positions-bounds-and-indices) output rules hold for both rungs. For a fast mesh:

- **`positions`.** The boundary contours' S1 polygon vertices, in contour order, reversed where FR6 reverses a contour (FR8), bit for bit.
- **`indices`.** FR7's triangles in emission order, counter-clockwise.
- **`boundaryEdges`.** V2 section f's once-only scan (`collect_boundary`), applied to the fast triangles in output order. No cell-ladder refinement runs: a fast mesh is conforming by construction, as SL-O2's argument shows.
- **`bounds`.** The exact component-wise minimum and maximum of `positions`, as V2.

**AL-FR9, the always-on output check.** It closes the spike's open obligation SL-OB4: a production contract needs either a proof of FR7 or an always-on output check. Before publication, the kernel checks:

1. every triangle's exact `orient2d` is strictly positive (FR7 already checks this);
2. no directed edge occurs twice: this is the existing duplicate-directed-edge check of V2's `collect_boundary` (`fill_export.rs:544-545`), run on the fast triangles. On an exact-rung mesh its failure stays a self-check failure (batch `internal-error`); on a fast mesh it maps to `MESH_CHECK`;
3. the edges used exactly once, as directed pairs, equal exactly the directed edges of the boundary contours that FR6 derived, oriented with the filled side on the left.

By the [SL-O2 argument](p3-b0-spike-ladder-contract.md#42-sl-o2-exact-region-and-mesh-validity), items 1 and 3 imply that the triangles have disjoint interiors, form a conforming mesh and cover exactly the region bounded by FR6's boundary contours as FR6 oriented them. That is the fast rung's counterpart of V2's ledger cross-check: the triangulation and the boundary chain must agree.

**What AL-FR9 does not check.** It takes FR6's boundary contours as given. Whether they are the right ones, so that the tiled region is R(P) under the fill rule, rests on FR6's correctness: SL-T2's argument, the fixtures SL-F6 and SL-F7, and SL-O2 in tests, which derives the boundary contours independently from exact shoelace orientations and ray-crossing nesting on every dumped path. In production FR6 is not re-derived per path (AL-OB9). The cost is a sort of at most 3 · (n + 2h) directed edges.

A failure is fallback `MESH_CHECK`, not `INTERNAL_ERROR`. The exact rung then produces a certified result, so a defective fast mesh is never published and the path is not lost. The check is unreachable under the textbook proof of FR7. Every test and benchmark gate in section 4 requires a `MESH_CHECK` count of zero, so a defect cannot hide behind the fallback.

### 3.6 Fallback reasons: are they observable?

**Not through the port.** The port result, the framing and the adapter carry no rung and no reason. V2 has no statistics export, and adding one would be a port or ABI change, which this contract does not make. What a user sees for a fallback path is the exact rung's result, unchanged.

**Natively, yes.** `fill_export::build_path` is `pub(crate)`. Its outcome gains the rung and, on fallback, the reason and the exact rung's topology selection. Native tests, the identity tool's native layer and the AL-S1 harness read it. In the browser, the rung mix is checked indirectly: AL-S2 requires the WASM output bytes to equal the native ones per cell (section 5.2), and the native run records the rung of every path.

**`cfg(test)` hooks.** `FillEngine`'s existing `TestHooks` gains `exact_only`, which skips rung 0, and the spike's fault switch gains the production seeded defects (section 4.3). Neither exists in a non-test build.

### 3.7 The exact rung as fallback and oracle

- **Fallback.** Rung 1 is V2's cascade, unchanged, including the cell-ladder refinement, the once-only boundary scan and the ledger cross-check (V2 section f). On every fallback path the export output is byte-identical to revision 1's (AL-I2).
- **Oracle.** The four `attempt*` entries stay bit-identical to the merge base (AL-I1). The exact rung stays callable in tests through `exact_only`, and the permanent oracle SL-O3 compares it with the fast rung on every path both admit (section 4.2).
- **Coverage of the exact rung's own paths.** With the ladder in front, some V2 tests would no longer reach the exact rung. Two rules keep its coverage:
  - Existing tests in `fill_export_tests.rs` keep their assertions, with two kinds of exception, both listed in the PR. First, a test whose assertion depends on the exact rung being taken on a path the fast rung admits sets `exact_only` on its engine; the edit is one line per engine construction. Second, the literal revision assertion `assert_eq!(FILL_V0_REVISION, 1)` (`fill_export_tests.rs:402`) changes to 2, together with the two literal lines of `tests/geometry/fill-adapter.test.ts` (section 4.4).
  - A sibling test asserts the ladder's behavior on the same input.
- **Fixtures that must stay on the exact rung.** A native test asserts that the C15 hanging fixtures (touching squares), V2's `Unresolved` and `KnotMismatch` controls, F04, F05, F07, F10 and F11 fall back on rung 0. Each expected reason is fixed in the test before its first execution; for the touching and overlapping squares it is `CROSSING`. This keeps the cell-ladder refinement and the cascade exercised through the export. If one of them is fast-admitted, the test names a replacement fixture that falls back, chosen before the change is run.

### 4.4 Identity rules and the re-baseline

Three rules fix what must stay identical and against what:

- **AL-I1, exact entries.** `CubicFillWorkspace::attempt`, `attempt_transverse`, `attempt_mixed_transverse` and `attempt_triangle_free` are bit-identical to the merge base on every case of `pnpm test:p3-fill-identity`'s native layer, in every unit. This is B0R's identity rule ([B0R section 2](p3-b0r-kernel-performance-contract.md#2-outcome-preservation-rule)), applied to the exact rung only.
- **AL-I2, fallback paths.** On every path where rung 0 falls back, the export output equals revision 1's, that is, the `exact_only` output. In a one-path batch the whole output is compared byte for byte. In a multi-path batch the comparison is per path: the status, every record field except `blockOffset`, and the block bytes, because a block's offset depends on the sizes of the blocks before it, which fast meshes change. This is checked natively per path (AL-F7) and, in AL-U3, against the merge base by the identity tool.
- **AL-I3, the fast rung against the spike.** On every path, the production rung 0 returns the same rung and reason as the frozen spike module, and on a fast outcome the same vertex bits and triangles. The only allowed differences are `RANGE` outside −511 ≤ e ≤ 510 and an AL-M5 `CLOSURE` wherever the spike passes FR3, whether the spike then admits the path or falls back later; each such difference is listed. No golden digest file is committed. After AL-U3 merges, the merge base carries revision 2, and later units compare against it in plain mode. This is decision AL-Q3.

### 4.5 Region and precision obligations

- **The 0.0625 row (BC2).** The private contract asks for "region preservation plus a bound on generated boundary displacement". SL-T1 and SL-T2 preserve orientation, nesting and the winding structure (spike 3.5). The fast mesh's region is exactly R(P): AL-FR9 checks on every path that the triangles tile the region of FR6's boundary contours, and SL-O2 checks in tests, against an independent derivation of the boundary contours, that this region is R(P) (AL-OB9). Its generated boundary displacement is therefore zero. The fast rung consumes none of the 0.0625 share.
- **The 0.125 curve share.** SL-C1 certifies δ̄(e) < δ per edge, including knot errors. On contracted contours, AL-M5 certifies the published boundary within δ (section 3.3).
- **Float32 storage and projection, 0.0625.** Not changed and not claimed (AL-OB2). The fast rung's micro-closure vertices k and m are about one Float64 ulp apart. In V3's packed binary32 offsets they may coincide, which leaves a zero-area needle that draws nothing. That is within the storage share's meaning, but it is a sub-pixel needle case of the kind the parked T04 line studies (AL-OB4).
- **A04 and D8.** A04 is evidenced by B4's measured error on a frozen corpus ([D8](p3-d8-parallel-tracks.md#roadmap-changes-adopted-with-d8)). This contract makes no A04 claim, and precision stays UNVERIFIED on every report. B4 measures the export as it stands then, revision 2 included.
- **Slivers, R0 and R2 (BC18).** A Steiner-free monotone triangulation makes slivers. On the spike's decision cells the fast meshes still had a smallest ρ about 180 times larger than the exact rung's, and fewer triangles below ρ = 0.1 (36% against 71%) ([record, SL-O5](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-18_p3_p3-b0-spike-ladder-v1_native_desktop-dkvusav_20261018T213619Z-89016cc9.md#sl-o5-sliver-statistics-fast-versus-exact)). R0 (clearance-aware triangulation) and R2 (K1 tiling with E1-E3) remain directions. FR7 does not preclude them: its output is an ordinary conforming triangle mesh, which K1 can clip per cell. A later clearance-aware diagonal choice would be a new revision. SL-O5 stays reported in every AL-S1 record; no R0 or R2 compliance is claimed (AL-OB4).

## 5. The performance gate

The gate has three parts, the user's three items: (a) the certificate optimization; (b) B0-W; (c) the product-stage verification. Parts (b) and (c) produce one verdict under B0's rule.

### 5.1 (a) Certificate-stage optimization

**Why.** In the spike's diagnostic run, the certificate FR1-FR5 takes 76% (P2/s=16), 78% (P4/s=16) and 82% (P8/s=8) of the fast rung's median, the S1 prefix 9-15%, and classification and triangulation together 9-10% ([record](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-18_p3_p3-b0-spike-ladder-v1_native_desktop-dkvusav_20261018T213619Z-89016cc9.md#diagnostic-stage-split)).

**Planning arithmetic (estimate, not a threshold).** With f = 0.82, the P8/s=8 share and the binding series, and the rest unchanged, the certificate must become c times faster for an overall factor F = 1 / ((1 − f) + f/c).

- F = 1.285 brings the spike's T̂ to B_init at k = 2. It needs c ≥ 1.37.
- At c = 2, F ≈ 1.69, which would tolerate a measured k up to about 2.6.

These ignore the export's own overhead (framing, boundary scan, output writing), which AL-S1 adds, and the noise that made check 2 fail twice. The budgets and the rule decide; this arithmetic sets no target.

**Rule: outcome identity.** Every optimization keeps the fast rung's outcomes bit-identical: for every input, the same rung, the same reason, and on a fast outcome the same output bytes. The stage order, the check order inside each stage, the thresholds, and the proved/not-proved decision of every check are unchanged. Logical counters (sweep candidates, clearance steps) are diagnostics; they may change and are reported. The `operationId` and revision stay, as B0R's did.

**Allowed classes.** Each optimization names its class, with its argument as a code comment:

| Class | What                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Argument required                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| AL-C1 | Same-arithmetic restructuring: hoisting values computed by the same binary64 operations on the same operands (for example v = t − s and \|v\|² per edge in FR5, or the control differences R_{m+1} − R_m shared by FR2 and FR4); loop fusion; removing the spike's copy of the S1 view into inline arrays                                                                                | Each decided quantity is computed by the identical sequence of IEEE 754 operations                                                                                                                                   |
| AL-C2 | Prefix-shared subdivision in FR2. The spike's `restrict` subdivides each leaf from the source cubic, following the high-to-low bits of n − 1, so every leaf costs O(d). Leaves of one cubic share bit prefixes, and a level-j interval control depends only on the first j bits. Caching each level's result along the leaf order computes the same intervals in O(1) amortized per leaf | The cached value at level j is the result of the same `split_half` calls on the same inputs as the uncached path                                                                                                     |
| AL-C3 | Dominance filters: a cheaper binary64 test that decides only when the interval test provably decides the same way, and otherwise runs the interval test unchanged. Example: a dot-product sign in FR4 accepted when \|fl(x)\| exceeds a proved a-priori bound on the interval's half-width                                                                                               | A per-site proof that the filter's "proved" implies the interval's "proved". Its "undecided" always runs the interval test, so it can never accept a case the interval rejects or reject a case the interval accepts |
| AL-C4 | Storage and control-flow work with no arithmetic: avoiding re-zeroing, smaller index types, branch layout, the merge-sort buffers                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | None beyond the identity oracle                                                                                                                                                                                      |

**Forbidden.** Reordering checks across or within stages; replacing an interval test by a different formula or operation order outside AL-C3; any epsilon, threshold or tolerance change; deciding a true zero; anything that would turn a fallback into a fast outcome or the reverse. Such a change would be a new operation revision with its own contract.

**Oracles for the optimization.**

1. AL-I3, the differential against the frozen spike module, over the corpus of section 4.4.
2. `pnpm test:p3-fill-identity` in plain mode against the pre-optimization merge base, cascade and WASM layers.
3. SL-O1 and SL-O2 in `pnpm test:geometry`.
4. All seeded defects still detected.
5. Zero allocations in a call.

A difference in any is a defect that blocks the merge.

**Process.**

- **AL-U4a.** A `cfg(test)` sub-stage split, nested stops inside FR2 to FR5, timed on the spike's diagnostic cells. It is a functional measurement that locates the cost, never performance evidence; its output goes into the PR.
- **AL-U4b and later.** One PR per optimization, or per tightly coupled group, light-tier under this contract's rule, each with one independent diff review. An AL-C3 filter's proof gets the reviewer's specific check.
- **Stop.** Optimization stops when the Primary judges further work unlikely to pay. The gate is measured once, by AL-S1 and AL-S2, not after each optimization.

### 5.2 (b) B0-W: the WASM/native ratio

B0's contract defines B0-W as "the same corpus bytes through the V2 adapter in headed Chrome and Edge, and the measured WASM/native ratio replacing k", its own scenario version and runner under B0's rule ([B0 section 6](p3-b0-feasibility-benchmark-contract.md#6-native-and-wasm-what-each-run-may-claim)). This contract fixes it as scenario **AL-S2**:

- **Identity.** Scenario `p3-b0-wasm`, version 1, runner `p3-b0-wasm-runner/1`, command `pnpm benchmark:p3-b0-wasm --profile functional|reference --environment-json <observations.json>`. Configuration hashed as in [B0 section 7](p3-b0-feasibility-benchmark-contract.md#7-environment-memory-categories-and-result-records), with the reference hash recorded in the plan when AL-U6 merges.
- **What runs.** The production operation `'geometry-wasm/fill-v0+r2'`, from a clean committed tree that contains AL-U3 to AL-U6: `main` under AL-Q7 (a), the committed head of the stacked branch under AL-Q7 (b).
  - **Browser leg.** The release WASM built by `pnpm build`, whose SHA-256 is verified in the page, through `createFillMeshPort` in a Vite production composition page. This follows the [P2.5 runner](p2-benchmark-runner.md#measurement-implementation): stable installed Chrome, then Edge; headed; one worker; a fresh page per repetition; no DevTools, tracing or video.
  - **Native leg.** In the same runner session on the same machine, just before the browsers: the AL-S1 harness's ratio mode (section 5.3) on the same cells.
- **Corpus and cells.** The B0 framed corpus bytes, SHA-256 `206cf6e9…9796`, decoded in the page into port requests (`toleranceExponent` −3, nonzero, built once outside timing). The 34 decision cells, paths 0-9 of each.
- **Timed sample.** R = 100 consecutive `buildFillMeshes` calls on the cell's 10-request array. The clock runs from before the first call to after the last result's checksum fold. The checksum folds every result's status, counts, position bits, indices and boundary pairs. The native leg times R = 100 consecutive `FillEngine::process` calls on the cell's framed input, and its window likewise ends after the fold of the same checksum over the decoded output of the last call, so both sides include one fold per call. The browser figure therefore includes the adapter's packing, copies and checks; the native one is the kernel export only. The ratio prices the whole port, which is what the product pays.
- **Sampling.** Per cell, 3 warm-up and 20 timed samples, the cell order shuffled per repetition as in B0, and 5 repetitions per browser and for the native leg. Clock quantum as P2.5: the minimum positive `performance.now` increment, observed before sampling. A cell whose sample is shorter than 100 quanta is UNVERIFIED. The adapter's arenas are provisioned in an untimed preparation sample; a reserve, growth or retry during a timed sample invalidates the repetition.
- **Ratio.** Per cell and repetition, r = (browser per-path median) / (native per-path median). Per cell, k_cell = the median of r over the five repetitions. Then **k̂ = the maximum, over Chrome and Edge, of k_cell at the largest admitted cell of each primary series** (P2/s=16, P4/s=16, P8/s=8). These are the cells nearest the extrapolation target. Every k_cell is reported. k̂ is labelled in the record as **browser port time over native export time in the test-harness build**: it is not a pure WASM/native code ratio, because the browser side includes the adapter.
- **One-path ratio, report-only.** Ten-path batches amortize the fixed cost of a port call (validation, packing, the kernel call, header checks), so k̂ may understate the browser cost of one edited path. The runner therefore also times, at the same three largest cells, R = 1,000 consecutive one-path calls on path 0, on both legs with the same window rule, and forms k̂₁ the same way. B0's rule uses one factor k for both estimates, and that rule is unchanged (BC16, SL-U5), so **k̂₁ does not enter the verdict**. The record reports t̂_p95 · k̂₁ beside the deciding t̂_edit as context, and states this limit of the edit proxy.
- **Validity, all required.**
  - Every browser and native repetition completes.
  - Every cell's browser checksum equals the native checksum. WASM output equals native bit for bit, which also confirms the native rung mix in the browser.
  - No primary largest cell is UNVERIFIED.
  - No reserve or growth during timing.
  - Metadata is complete: the [benchmark README](../benchmarks/README.md)'s browser fields, the WASM SHA-256 and the adapter version.
- **Memory observation.** `linearMemoryBytes()` per browser after the run, against BC19's 256 MB ceiling. It is not a decision input.
- **Not claimed.** GPU, draw, fringe builder or end-to-end editor cost.

### 6.4 Risks

- **The gate may miss again.** The spike's T̂ is 1.29 × B_init at k = 2, and k is unmeasured; a measured k above 2 widens the gap. The export adds overhead the spike did not have. The planning arithmetic of section 5.1 is an estimate.
- **INCONCLUSIVE again.** Check 2 failed twice on P8 p95 fits of individually timed paths. At the fast rung's per-path times (0.034-0.090 ms in the spike), a delay of a given absolute size weighs more. Under AL-Q5 (a) the sampling is unchanged, so a third INCONCLUSIVE is plausible. It would go to the user under B0-Q3.
- **Optimization yield.** The certificate's internal split is unmeasured (AL-U4a measures it). Bit-identical outcomes rule out the largest algorithmic changes.
- **Admission change in practice.** Generic-coordinate paths now publish (AL-Q2). Their meshes are certified by the same proof and oracle-checked on the dump sets, but beyond the B0 ring family their admission and their look are unobserved (AL-OB3).
- **Slivers and needles.** Thin fast triangles bear on the A5 fringe's features and on R0. Micro-closure needles may collapse in binary32 (section 4.5).
- **Line-only paths.** Under AL-Q4 (a), line paths change meshes too. Their regions are equal or within the τ-band, but V4 screenshots and B4 measure the new meshes.
- **Test churn.** Existing `fill_export_tests.rs` tests need `exact_only`. A careless edit could weaken an assertion; the reviewer checks that each edit is only the hook.
- **Double code.** The spike module and the production module implement the same algorithm. The spike is frozen and test-only; AL-I3 keeps them consistent, and the spike can be retired by a later decision once AL-I3 is no longer wanted.
- **Out of scope but real.** The zoom-step rebuild (DF9), the fringe builder (DF8), draw cost and lower-tier hardware.
