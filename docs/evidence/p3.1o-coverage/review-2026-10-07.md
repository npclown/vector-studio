## Findings

**1. A2/A5 interior and seam deficit of 30/255.**

- **Where:** only the four `carrier/Z/depth-positive/{star,zero-closure}:{nonzero,evenodd}/S8` rows (77, 83, 89 and 95). At 1x, pixels (150,8) and (149,9) are observed at 225. At 4x, 4 pixels are observed at 240 and 247.
- **Cause:** the line-not-segment rule, which follows from the contract's half-plane distance and its "no corner rule" observation choice. Both pixel centers lie in triangle [1,4,5], whose only flagged edge is boundary edge 4-5. Its interior angle at vertex 4 is about 174°. At (150.5, 8.5) the line distance is −0.5406, which gives a ramp of 0.882, or 225/255. The segment distance is more than 3 px, so the oracle correctly classes these pixels as interior.
- **Status:** this is behavior by construction, not an implementation defect. A1 is immune because its interior output is 1. Any adopted ramp needs a segment-aware or corner rule (Q3).

**2. Nondeterminism in A1 4x crops.**

- **Run 1 against run 2:** Chrome differs only in row 76; Edge differs in rows 76 and 88.
- **Chrome against Edge in run 1:** rows 82 and 88.
- **Where the difference is:** every difference is in an A1 4x main crop. It is 1 LSB, at y = 19, x = 294-297, on 2 to 4 pixels. All 1x and diagnostic passes are identical.
- **Inputs:** rows 76, 82, 88 and 94 upload byte-identical soups, yet resolve differently from each other within one run.
- **What it is:** each affected pixel's exact resolve mean (for example (255 + 3·84)/4 = 126.75) flips between round-to-nearest and one lower.
- **What it is not:** rasterization order and blending, since `max` is order-independent.
- **Plausible cause:** the implementation-defined 4x resolve or storage path, a stated contract limit. This is not proven.
- **Handling:** runs are not averaged. The aggregates differ by at most 1/255 in two rows.
