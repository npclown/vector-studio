## Findings

**1. Boundary-shift outlier, +0.28 px at DPR 1.5.**

- **Rows.** It occurs in eight rows with the same tip geometry: rows 73, 79, 85 and 91 (`carrier/Z/depth-positive/.../S2`) and rows 38, 47, 56 and 65 (`carrier/Y/triangle-free/.../S2`).
- **Why the denominator is small.** Only an acute convex tip reaches the 3-px inset window, so just 0.93 px of boundary length falls inside it.
- **The pixel.** The single contributing pixel is (3,3). Its center is outside the tip, so `c = 0.3695` and obs = 94, against a reference area of about 0.11.
- **Cause.** A small-denominator artifact plus the expected over-coverage of the distance rule at an acute tip. It is not a systematic bias.

**2. Thin-feature over-coverage.** THIN-Z1 at DPR 1.5 has a maximum |obs − ref| of 0.318 at pixel (15,16) and an area of 38.0, against a reference of 28.1.

- At this DPR, a row of pixel centers lies inside the 0.469-px strip, so the nearest-edge rule gives `c ≥ 0.5` along it.
- This is the thin-feature and medial-axis behavior the contract names. Partial-coverage tolerance is deferred, so it is an observation, not a gate.

**3. 1x equals 4x.** `c` is evaluated once per pixel at the center and is bit-identical across primitives. G3 shows that every sample of a near pixel is covered exactly once, so the 4x resolve is exact.
