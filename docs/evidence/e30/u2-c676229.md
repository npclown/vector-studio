## Conditional levers (E30-A07, section 4.3)

- **L3, backend encode or GPU: condition met before L1 and L2; decided after U3 and U4's PC re-read.** Section 4.3 measures L3 after L1 and L2. Today `vs-submit` p95 is 31.6 to 47.7 ms in the all-visible rows (4.6 to 5.3 ms in the typical view, up to 5.3 ms on Chrome), and `vs-gpu` p95 is 51 to 370 ms everywhere except stress. `vs-gpu` includes the wait for the main thread to resolve the completion, which L1 and L2 shorten, so how much of it remains is known only after them. If it remains, U5 profiles the backend and GPU on the PC; a remedy that needs a new pipeline, render bundle or draw-order change is written up as its own item first.
- **L4, the frame after an isolation: called for.** Outside isolation and not flagged as the font load, the busy runs have 13 long tasks over 50 ms in 12 of 20 runs, of 52 to 74 ms. In all 13 a single `vs-prepare` of 39 to 57 ms lies inside the task, and 12 of them start 2 to 75 ms after an isolation ends (the 13th 2.8 s after). Every busy run's prepare maximum is 44.5 to 58.8 ms, against a p95 of 8 to 11.3 ms. These are the frames after an isolation that section 4.3 names; U5 takes them under L2's rule.
- **L5, the renderer's share of latency: called for, at the margin.** The sum of the per-run medians of `vs-prepare` and `vs-submit` is 10.0 to 11.0 ms (6.0 to 6.9 plus 3.9 to 4.2), just over the 10 ms threshold; it is a sum of medians, not the median of the sum, and it lies within the 0.6 to 0.8 ms observer effect Chrome's view-edit rows may show. The scene set apply sits inside the 10 to 11 ms handler as well. U5 takes it under L2. The larger share, claim to done, goes with L3.
